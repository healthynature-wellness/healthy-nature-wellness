PLANT LIBRARY / BOTANICAL PROFILE

Turmeric

Curcuma longa

Known for its vivid golden color and earthy flavor, turmeric is a culinary rhizome with a complex history.

Evidence note: This profile is educational. It does not establish that this plant treats, prevents, or cures disease. Evidence may be limited and specific to particular preparations.

Botanical overview

Turmeric is a perennial plant in the ginger family. The yellow-orange rhizome is used fresh or dried and is common in many South Asian cuisines.

Nutritional properties & naturally occurring compounds

Turmeric contains curcuminoids, including curcumin, along with volatile oils and other plant compounds. Culinary amounts differ from high-dose extracts.

Traditional uses

Turmeric has a long history of culinary and traditional use in South Asia. Traditional applications vary and should be understood in cultural context.

What does research say?

Curcumin and turmeric preparations have been studied for several health outcomes. Research quality and results vary, and bioavailability differs substantially between products. No supplement should be assumed to prevent or cure disease.

Common preparations

Use turmeric in curries, soups, rice, marinades, and warm drinks. Supplements and enhanced-absorption formulations are not equivalent to culinary use.

Safety considerations

Concentrated supplements may cause gastrointestinal symptoms and have been associated with rare liver injury, particularly some enhanced-bioavailability products. Consult a clinician if you have gallbladder problems or take medicines.
