Elderberry

Sambucus nigra

The dark berries of elder are used in traditional foods and cooked preparations, but correct identification and preparation are essential.

Evidence note: This profile is educational. It does not establish that this plant treats, prevents, or cures disease. Evidence may be limited and specific to particular preparations.

Botanical overview

European elder is a shrub or small tree. Its flowers and ripe berries have culinary uses in some traditions. Other Sambucus species exist, so identification matters.

Nutritional properties & naturally occurring compounds

Ripe elderberries contain pigments such as anthocyanins and other polyphenols. Composition varies by species and preparation.

Traditional uses

Elder flowers and berries appear in traditional foods, syrups, and seasonal preparations. Traditional use should not be confused with evidence that elderberry treats viral infections.

What does research say?

Clinical studies have investigated elderberry products for cold and flu symptoms, but evidence remains limited and product-specific. It is not a substitute for vaccination, antiviral treatment, or medical advice.

Common preparations

Only correctly identified, ripe berries should be used, and they should be cooked thoroughly. Do not experiment with raw plant parts.

Safety considerations

Raw or unripe elderberries, leaves, stems, and roots can contain compounds that may cause nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. Never consume these parts. Consult a clinician before supplements if pregnant, breastfeeding, immunocompromised, or taking medicines.
