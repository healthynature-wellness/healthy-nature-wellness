PLANT LIBRARY / BOTANICAL PROFILE

Rose Hips

Rosa spp.

The fruit of the rose, rose hips are used in teas, syrups, and preserves.

Evidence note: This profile is educational. It does not establish that this plant treats, prevents, or cures disease. Evidence may be limited and specific to particular preparations.

Botanical overview

Rose hips are the accessory fruits of rose plants. Species, growing conditions, ripeness, and processing influence appearance and composition. Fine hairs and seeds inside are typically removed during food preparation.

Nutritional properties & naturally occurring compounds

Rose hips can contain vitamin C, carotenoids, polyphenols, and fiber. Levels vary widely and can be reduced by storage, heat, and processing.

Traditional uses

Rose hips have been gathered in many regions and used in foods, teas, jams, and traditional preparations. Food use is distinct from concentrated supplements.

What does research say?

Research has examined certain rose hip preparations for specific outcomes, including joint-related symptoms, but evidence is product-specific and not conclusive for broad health claims.

Common preparations

Rose hips may be dried for tea or cooked into syrups, jellies, and sauces. Remove seeds and irritating inner hairs carefully; gather only correctly identified plants from clean areas.

Safety considerations

Do not eat rose hips unless correctly identified and prepared. Avoid plants sprayed with pesticides. Discuss concentrated supplements with a clinician if pregnant, breastfeeding, managing a condition, or taking medication.
